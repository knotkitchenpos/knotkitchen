# KnotKitchen Restaurant Service Agreement — Legal and Commercial Review (India)

Review date: 16 September 2026
Source document: `AGREEMENT_TEMPLATE` v1.0 in the KnotKitchen onboarding portal (agreement.knotkitchen.com), together with Annexure A as generated by the portal.
Reviewer role: acting as Indian commercial-contract and technology/SaaS counsel. This is a drafting review, not a legal opinion; see Part G for the items an advocate, chartered accountant and company secretary must confirm before production use.

---

## PART A — Executive Legal Review

**1. The contracting party "KnotKitchen" is not identified.** The agreement names "KnotKitchen" with no legal name, entity type, registered office, GSTIN or PAN. A contract with an unidentified party is hard to enforce, cannot support a GST tax invoice, and gives the Restaurant no address for notices. Placeholders are inserted; the legal name, entity type and GSTIN must be filled from KnotKitchen's actual registration.

**2. The Installation Charge is internally contradictory.** Clause 5 lists ₹2,000 / ₹2,000 / ₹3,000 and says the amount is "refundable according to the termination terms below"; clause 6.6 refunds 50% before 6 months and 100% after; clause 6.4 says commitment amounts are "non-refundable"; clause 4 refunds Wallet balance. The instructed commercial rule (₹2,000 / ₹2,500 / ₹3,500; 25% refund before 12 completed months, 100% after) replaces all of this. The charge is drafted as a refundable one-time Installation and Setup Charge, not a security deposit: it pays for installation, configuration and onboarding, and, where a printer option is chosen, the provision of KnotKitchen-owned Equipment on loan. Its GST and accounting characterisation must be confirmed (Part G, item 7).

**3. The Early Termination Charge (clause 6.2) carries enforceability risk. DECISION (17 Sept 2026): replaced by repayment of the discount received (clause 6.5 of the deployed v2.0 text).** Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 allows recovery of a stipulated sum only as reasonable compensation for loss actually suffered (Kailash Nath Associates v. DDA, (2015) 4 SCC 136). "50% of remaining discounted charges" is a plausible pre-estimate of lost margin on a subscription commitment, and the existing commercial model relies on it, so it is preserved. It is redrafted as a genuine pre-estimate, capped, made mutually exclusive with any discount clawback, and flagged for KnotKitchen to decide between (a) the existing Early Termination Charge and (b) a lighter "discount repayment" model. The agreement is not silent on the point, so no new penalty is invented.

**4. Commitment versus 7-day termination.** Clause 6.6 lets either Party terminate on 7 days' notice, which contradicts the minimum commitment. Resolved: the Restaurant may terminate on notice at any time, but termination before the end of a Commitment Period triggers the Early Termination Charge; KnotKitchen's termination for convenience needs 30 days' notice and refunds unused prepaid fees.

**5. Suspension and inactivity are under-specified.** A "24-hour grace period" then "suspend/lock" is the only process, with no reminder, no suspension notice, no rule on data, domain, Equipment, commitment or eventual termination. A full Inactive Account process is added (clause 13), with reasonable notice before permanent termination and lawful exceptions for fraud, security abuse and illegal activity.

**6. Data protection is one paragraph and mis-describes roles.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is in force; the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 with phased commencement (Board provisions immediate; consent-manager rules at 12 months; the substantive obligations, including notice, security, breach intimation and erasure, from 13 May 2027). For end-customer data collected through the Restaurant's website and POS, the Restaurant is the Data Fiduciary and KnotKitchen its Data Processor under a valid contract (section 8(2)); for the Restaurant's own account data, KnotKitchen is the Data Fiduciary. Clause 8 and Schedule 2 are rewritten on this footing, without promising absolute security and without asserting KnotKitchen "owns" customer personal data.

**7. GST drafting is thin and partly wrong by omission.** Nothing states whether prices are exclusive of GST, how the commitment discount interacts with taxable value, place of supply, the Restaurant's GSTIN, or credit notes on refund. Under section 15(3)(a) CGST Act a discount recorded on the invoice at the time of supply reduces taxable value; a discount given later needs a credit note under section 34 (the section 15(3)(b) pre-agreement condition is being removed by the Finance Act, 2026 amendment, effective from a date to be notified). The draft makes the Commitment Discount a time-of-supply discount shown on the invoice, so GST is computed on the discounted subscription fee. The 18% rate for IT/SaaS services (SAC 9983) was unchanged by the September 2025 rate rationalisation, but no rate is hard-coded in the agreement.

**8. Electronic execution is mislabelled.** Clause 14 calls the process "electronic-signature/eSign" while the portal workflow is: checkbox acceptance in the portal, download of the generated PDF, signature outside the portal (Aadhaar eSign or a handwritten signature that is scanned), and upload. Under the Information Technology Act, 2000 only a digital signature (section 3) or a Second Schedule technique such as Aadhaar e-KYC eSign (section 3A; Electronic Signature or Electronic Authentication Technique and Procedure Rules, 2015; CCA e-authentication guidelines) is a statutory "electronic signature". A checkbox or typed name is not; it is evidence of assent to a contract formed electronically under section 10A. Clause 14 is rewritten to describe exactly what is done, and Schedule 3 lists the audit trail the portal must keep so the record can be proved under section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (in force 1 July 2024; certificate with hash value required for electronic records tendered as evidence).

**9. Stamp duty is not addressed.** Electronic execution does not remove stamp duty. Duty on an agreement is a State matter (for example, Maharashtra expressly stamps electronic records; West Bengal applies the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 as amended for the State). The draft records that stamping/e-stamping is handled under the law of the relevant State and does not assume a nationwide position.

**10. Liability clause is one-sided and incomplete.** Clause 16 lists exclusions with no cap, no direct/indirect distinction and no carve-outs. Replaced with a proportionate limitation, a defined cap and statutory carve-outs. Indemnities, force majeure, IP, confidentiality exceptions, notices, amendments, assignment and survival are added.

**11. Marketplace and restaurant responsibilities need precision.** Clause 9 is adequate as a general third-party clause but says nothing specific about Swiggy/Zomato; clause 7 is a list without the food-law allocation the instructions require. Both are expanded; nothing is disclaimed that the law leaves with KnotKitchen.

**12. Domain names are not addressed at all.** A new clause 11A implements the `.in` first-year-free (where applicable) / `.com` chargeable model, with renewal responsibility and registrar rules.

**13. Consumer Protection Act, 2019.** A restaurant buying software for a commercial purpose is not a "consumer" (section 2(7), explanation (a)), so the Act does not govern the KnotKitchen–Restaurant relationship. It does govern the Restaurant's relationship with diners; the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 may treat the Restaurant, as the seller operating an online ordering website, as an e-commerce entity. The draft allocates diner-facing obligations to the Restaurant and keeps KnotKitchen liable for failures of its own service. No clause purports to waive non-waivable statutory rights.

**14. Dispute resolution.** Courts of Kolkata alone is workable but slow and, for an all-India customer base, open to territorial objections where no cause of action arises in Kolkata. The draft adds arbitration (sole arbitrator, seat Kolkata, Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as amended to date; the 2024 draft Amendment Bill has not been enacted), preserves section 9 interim relief and statutory forums, and keeps Kolkata courts for matters outside arbitration. KnotKitchen should confirm seat and whether it prefers court litigation for small claims (Part G, item 5).

**15. App and agreement are currently out of step.** The onboarding portal's "Installation & Equipments" page (printer and commitment selection) was removed on 16 September 2026 at KnotKitchen's request, and the portal's amounts (₹2,000/₹2,000/₹3,000) differ from the instructed ₹2,000/₹2,500/₹3,500. The revised agreement requires both selections to be made in a KnotKitchen application and recorded in the Commercial Schedule. Either the portal page is restored with the new amounts, or the selection moves to the POS/CSD activation flow. Part F lists the implementation work.

No statement is made that the agreement is "fully compliant". Material uncertainties are listed in Part G.

## 1. SERVICES

1.1 KnotKitchen provides restaurant technology and related services, which may include:

- Cloud-based POS for order management, billing and e-bills.
- Dedicated restaurant website for online ordering, where included in the selected Plan.
- Supported third-party integrations such as Swiggy, Zomato and other platforms, subject to clause 9A.
- Google Business Profile management and local SEO assistance, where selected.
- Equipment provided under clause 5.

1.2 Services available to the Restaurant depend on the selected Subscription Plan and any add-ons, as shown in the KnotKitchen Application and recorded in the Commercial Schedule.

1.3 KnotKitchen is a technology and software provider. KnotKitchen does not prepare, manufacture, package, store, sell or deliver the Restaurant's food, and is not the seller of any food or goods ordered through the Services, unless a separate written service arrangement expressly says otherwise (for example, delivery arranged through KnotKitchen under clause 4.2).

## 1A. AVAILABILITY, MAINTENANCE AND SERVICE CHANGES

1A.1 KnotKitchen will use commercially reasonable efforts to keep the Services available. The Services depend on the internet, the Restaurant's devices and connectivity, cloud infrastructure and other Third-Party Services, and KnotKitchen does not promise uninterrupted or error-free operation. No uptime service level is offered under this Agreement. If KnotKitchen publishes a service-level document for a Plan, that document applies only to the extent it expressly says so and, in case of conflict, this Agreement prevails on liability.

1A.2 KnotKitchen may carry out planned maintenance, updates, patches, upgrades and security fixes. KnotKitchen will try to schedule planned maintenance outside peak restaurant hours and to give advance notice through the application for maintenance expected to interrupt the Services for more than [30] minutes. Emergency maintenance to address security or stability may be carried out without notice.

1A.3 KnotKitchen may modify, add or withdraw features. KnotKitchen will give at least 30 days' notice through the application or by email before withdrawing a feature that is material to the Restaurant's Plan, unless the withdrawal is required by law, security or a Third-Party Service change outside KnotKitchen's control. If a withdrawal materially reduces the Plan the Restaurant is paying for during a Commitment Period, the Restaurant may terminate under clause 12.2(c) without an Early Termination Charge.

A change in the Subscription Fee or Order Charges applies to the Restaurant only (a) after at least 30 days' notice by email and in the application, and (b) from the Renewal Date following the end of the notice period; and, during a Commitment Period, not before the Commitment Period ends. 2.5 The Restaurant may upgrade its Plan at any time; the higher Subscription Fee applies from the date of upgrade on a pro-rata basis for the current Billing Period, or from the next Renewal Date, as shown in the application at the time. The Restaurant may downgrade with effect from the next Renewal Date. Plan changes during a Commitment Period are governed by clause 6.4.

## 4. FEES, WALLET, INVOICING AND TAXES

4.1 **What the Restaurant pays.** The Restaurant pays, as applicable and as shown in the Commercial Schedule: (a) Subscription Fees; (b) the Installation Charge; (c) Order Charges; (d) Domain charges; (e) add-on and other separately billed KnotKitchen charges; (f) delivery charges where delivery is arranged through KnotKitchen; and (g) Taxes on each of the above. Each item is shown as a separate line in the application and on invoices.

4.2 **Delivery arranged by the Restaurant.** If the Restaurant independently arranges delivery, KnotKitchen will not charge a KnotKitchen delivery fee.

4.3 **Wallet.** KnotKitchen maintains a prepaid Wallet for the Restaurant. The Restaurant tops up the Wallet through the payment methods offered in the application. Amounts due under clause 4.1 are deducted from the Wallet on their due date. The due date for a Subscription Fee is the Renewal Date; for Order Charges, the date shown in the application (daily or periodic as configured); for the Installation Charge, the date of selection or Activation, whichever the application shows. The application shows the Wallet balance, each deduction and the next due date at all times.

4.4 **Insufficient balance.** If the Wallet has insufficient balance on a due date, clause 13 (Suspension and Inactive Accounts) applies. The 24-hour grace period in clause 13.2 is the first step of that process.

4.5 **Taxes and GST.**
(a) All charges are stated **exclusive of Taxes** unless the application expressly marks a price as inclusive. Taxes are charged in addition at the rate applicable on the date of supply and shown separately.
(b) Where KnotKitchen is registered under GST, KnotKitchen will issue a tax invoice (or, for advances, a receipt voucher and later a tax invoice) in accordance with the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the rules under it, showing KnotKitchen's GSTIN, the Restaurant's GSTIN if supplied, the taxable value, any discount recorded at the time of supply, and CGST/SGST or IGST as applicable. Where KnotKitchen is not registered under GST, no GST is charged and the invoice will say so. [Confirm KnotKitchen's registration status.]
(c) The Restaurant must supply an accurate GSTIN and legal name if it wishes to claim input tax credit, and must promptly update any change. KnotKitchen is not liable for any loss of credit, penalty or interest arising from GSTIN or address details that the Restaurant supplied incorrectly or failed to update.
(d) Place of supply is determined under the Integrated Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 using the Restaurant's address in this Agreement; the Restaurant must inform KnotKitchen of any change of address.

## PART G — Legal Qualification / Lawyer Review Items

These points are not settled by this review and must be confirmed by an Indian advocate, chartered accountant or company secretary before production use:

1. **Stamp duty and e-stamping.** Which State's stamp law applies (place of execution, Restaurant's State, KnotKitchen's State), the article and duty for a service agreement with a refundable charge and equipment loan, whether e-stamping is available, who bears the duty, and whether the portal should collect it. Electronic execution does not eliminate duty.

2. **GST classification and rate.** Confirm KnotKitchen's GST registration status and GSTIN; SAC classification of the subscription (likely 9983 IT/SaaS at 18%, unchanged after the September 2025 rate changes) and of Order Charges (commission/agency vs. software service); whether Wallet top-ups are advances requiring receipt vouchers; place-of-supply treatment for Restaurants in other States (IGST); and credit-note mechanics on refunds. Do not rely on the rate stated here without confirmation.

3. **Early Termination Charge.** Confirm that 50% of remaining discounted fees is defensible as a genuine pre-estimate under section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, or replace it with repayment of the discount actually received (which is more clearly compensatory and easier to explain to Restaurants). The draft keeps the existing model with a bracketed alternative.

4. **Installation Charge characterisation.** Confirm for GST and accounting whether the Installation Charge is (a) consideration for an installation/onboarding service plus an equipment loan (taxable when paid, refunded by credit note), or (b) partly a refundable deposit (not consideration until applied), and align the invoice and the refund credit-note treatment accordingly. The draft describes it as a refundable service charge, not a security deposit.

5. **Arbitration seat and forum.** Confirm Kolkata as seat, sole-arbitrator appointment mechanism, whether KnotKitchen prefers court litigation for small claims (arbitration cost may exceed claim value for small Restaurants), interaction with the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 (pre-institution mediation under section 12A for commercial suits), and the MSMED Act, 2006 if KnotKitchen or the Restaurant is a registered MSME supplier.

6. **Entity and signatory structure.** Fill KnotKitchen's legal name, entity type, registered office, PAN and GSTIN; confirm the sales agent's authority to sign for KnotKitchen (or whether KnotKitchen countersigns centrally); confirm the proof-of-authority documents to be requested from partnerships, LLPs and companies.

7. **DPDP implementation obligations as of the signing date.** Confirm which DPDP Rules provisions are in force at the signing date (phased commencement from 13 November 2025; substantive obligations from 13 May 2027), whether KnotKitchen could be notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary, breach-notification timelines under the Rules, and consent-notice wording for Restaurant websites. Confirm hosting location and any cross-border sub-processors.

8. **State-specific restaurant and licensing provisions.** Shop and establishment, trade licence, fire and municipal requirements vary by State and municipality; the draft places them on the Restaurant generally. Confirm whether the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 obligations of a seller apply to Restaurants using the KnotKitchen website, and adjust clause 11.4 display requirements.

9. **Domain ownership and registrar structure.** Confirm which registrar KnotKitchen uses, whether the Restaurant or KnotKitchen is registrant of record, the transfer process and lock periods, and .IN Registry policy requirements.

10. **Liability cap and carve-outs.** Confirm the cap period (six months of fees) and any minimum amount, and whether data-protection breaches should have a separate higher cap rather than an uncapped carve-out.

11. **Signature workflow.** Confirm whether KnotKitchen will integrate an Aadhaar eSign provider (licensed ESP under CCA guidelines) into the portal, or continue to accept scanned handwritten signatures; the evidential weight of the two differs.

12. **Interest on late payment.** Confirm the 12% figure and whether interest is charged at all.

13. **Retention periods.** Confirm 8 years for acceptance records, 30/60-day export and deletion windows, and 90-day backup cycle against KnotKitchen's actual systems and statutory retention (income-tax and GST record-keeping periods).
